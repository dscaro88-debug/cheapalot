Shipping E-Bike Lithium Batteries from China: UN38.3 & Sea Freight Rules

By CheapALot Team · August 25, 2026 · 7 min read · Electric Bikes & Mobility

Most e-bike import problems are not about the bike - they are about the battery. Lithium packs are classified as Class 9 dangerous goods, which means air freight is essentially unavailable, and a single missing document can hold an entire container at the port. This guide explains the four documents that gate your shipment, the sea-freight-only reality, and how to plan around it.

Why Batteries Change Everything

An e-bike without its battery is just a bicycle box; with the battery, it becomes a dangerous-goods shipment. Every lithium battery must travel with transport documentation that proves it is safe and correctly packed. Get this wrong and the shipper refuses the load, customs detains the container, or in the worst case a fire puts the whole vessel at risk. This is why battery logistics sit at the top of every serious e-bike importer's checklist.

The Four Documents That Gate Your Shipment

  1. UN38.3 test report - proves the battery cell/pack passed transport safety tests. Self-made reports are rejected by customs; it must come from a recognised testing body.
  2. MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet) - documents chemical composition and handling hazards.
  3. Dangerous-goods packaging certificate - the box/foam inserts meet Class 9 packaging rules.
  4. Shipping declaration - the carrier's dangerous-goods declaration and container packing statement.

Sea Freight Only: What That Means for Lead Time

In practice, e-bike batteries move by sea freight only - air capacity for Class 9 lithium is extremely limited and prohibitively expensive. Budget production of 45-60 days plus 25-40 days of sea transit to Europe or North America. That is why we start battery paperwork at order confirmation, not after production: the documents must be ready the day the container is packed.

Beyond the base freight, budget for: dangerous-goods surcharges from carriers, certified packaging materials, battery documentation costs, and potential battery/vehicle separation (some routes ship batteries separately). Frequently Asked Questions

Can e-bike batteries be shipped by air?

Practically no. Lithium batteries are Class 9 dangerous goods and air carriers impose severe restrictions. E-bikes are shipped by sea freight with UN38.3, MSDS and certified dangerous-goods packaging.

What is UN38.3?

UN38.3 is the United Nations transport test for lithium batteries. It verifies that cells and packs survive the shocks, vibration, temperature and short-circuit conditions of transport. Customs and carriers require the test report before shipping.

Do I need my own dangerous-goods licence to import e-bikes?

Not usually. Your supplier and freight forwarder handle the DG declaration and packaging. Your job is to confirm the paperwork exists and matches the shipment before it leaves the factory.

What happens if the battery documents are wrong?

The carrier may refuse the load, or customs may detain the container until the paperwork is corrected - which costs demurrage and can ruin your stock plan. Documentation must be verified before shipment, not at the port.
