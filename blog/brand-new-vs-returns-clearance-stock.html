Brand-New vs Returns: How to Tell If Clearance Stock Is Really New

By CheapALot Team · August 24, 2026 · 7 min read

"Clearance stock" is one of the most misused phrases in wholesale. It's used to describe everything from factory-fresh surplus at 60% off to bins of opened, returned, half-complete boxes. They are not the same product, and they should not be priced or sold the same way. Yet many first-time buyers only discover the difference after a container arrives.

This guide shows you how to verify that the clearance stock you're buying is genuinely brand new — and why it's worth paying attention to the difference.

The Two Very Different Products Called "Clearance"

In the market today you'll find two main grades of surplus inventory:

Brand-New Factory Surplus

Products that were over-produced or over-ordered: a factory made too many, a retailer ordered too much, an export order was cancelled at the last moment. The goods are new, in original packaging, never sold to a consumer. They're discounted because someone needs to move volume and free up cash — not because there's anything wrong with them.

  • Original sealed packaging, factory labels intact
  • Consistent quality across the entire batch
  • Sells through normal retail channels at near-full price
  • Typical retail margin: 2–5x landed cost

Returns, Refurbished & Damaged

Customer returns and reconditioned goods. These may be opened, lightly used, missing parts, or cosmetically imperfect. They're cheaper per unit — and they come with inherited problems: more complaints, returns to you, and a hard ceiling on what customers will pay. Some sales channels refuse them entirely.

  • Opened or re-sealed packaging, mixed condition
  • Inconsistent quality across the batch
  • Must be sold as "grade B" or clearance-grade, at a discount
  • Higher handling and complaint costs

Why "Brand New" Matters for Your Margin

Run the same order through both scenarios. A $10,000 order of brand-new surplus at 5% commission can retail at 3x landed cost through normal channels. The same $10,000 on returns-grade goods is capped at maybe 1.5x — and you'll eat refunds and unsellable units. The freight, the duty and your time are identical. The difference is profit.

There's also a brand argument. If you're building a discount retail name, every box your customer opens builds or breaks trust. A customer who receives an opened, returned item once won't come back — and may leave a review that follows you for years. New goods protect the reputation you're building.

4 Checks to Verify Stock Is Brand New

Before you pay, run these checks:

  1. Ask directly, in writing. "Is this factory surplus (brand new, original packaging) or returns-grade?" A legitimate supplier answers clearly. Vague answers are the first red flag.
  2. Check packaging. Original sealed packaging, factory labels, no re-sealing tape, consistent outer boxes. Returns-grade stock almost always shows handling wear.
  3. Demand photographed current stock. Photos of the actual lot, not catalogue images. Ask for close-ups of packaging and a few different units from the batch.
  4. Verify the supplier physically. A factory or a physical booth in Yiwu International Trade City — with an operating history measured in years — is a far stronger signal than an email-only seller.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between brand-new and returns clearance stock?

Brand-new clearance stock is over-produced or over-ordered factory surplus: new products in original packaging that were never sold to a consumer. Returns are goods customers sent back — possibly opened, used, or missing parts. Brand-new surplus carries full retail quality and resells at a higher price; returns carry quality risk and complaints.

How can I tell if clearance stock is brand new?

Check four things: original sealed packaging, factory labels and date codes, consistent condition across the batch, and the supplier's ability to show photographed current stock at a physical market booth. Ask directly whether the stock is factory surplus (new) or returns-grade — and get the answer in writing.

Why does buying brand-new clearance stock matter for my margins?

New goods sell at 2–5x landed cost and can be sold through normal retail channels at near-full price. Returns and refurbished goods are capped — buyers expect a discount, complaints are more frequent, and some channels refuse them entirely.
