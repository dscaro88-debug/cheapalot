Watch Specifications Explained: A Bulk Buyer's Guide to Materials & Features

By CheapALot Team · August 18, 2026 · 7 min read

When you're buying watches wholesale, the spec sheet is your best friend. Terms like "316L steel," "mineral crystal," and "3 ATM" can seem like jargon, but they directly determine quality, durability, customer satisfaction, and ultimately your return rate. This guide decodes every specification you'll encounter when sourcing watches from China.

Case Material: The Foundation of Quality

The case is the watch's body — it houses the movement, holds the glass, and attaches to the strap. Material quality is the single biggest indicator of overall build quality.

316L Stainless Steel (Standard Quality)

316L is surgical-grade stainless steel. The "L" stands for "low carbon," which makes it more corrosion-resistant and easier to polish. Key benefits:

  • Hypoallergenic: Low nickel content means it won't cause skin reactions
  • Corrosion-resistant: Won't rust or tarnish even with daily wear
  • Durable: Resists dents and scratches better than alloy alternatives
  • Industry standard: Used by mid-tier brands worldwide

All watches in our in-stock collection use 316L stainless steel cases — we don't sell anything less.

What to Avoid: Alloy and Zinc Cases

Some budget suppliers use zinc alloy or 304 stainless to cut costs. Warning signs:

  • Skin irritation: Higher nickel content causes green marks on skin
  • Corrosion: Cases pit and rust within 6-12 months
  • Weight: Alloys feel noticeably lighter than 316L steel
  • Finish wear: Plating chips off alloy cases quickly

Glass/Crystal: What Protects the Dial

The glass covering the dial is called the "crystal" in watchmaking. Three types dominate the wholesale market:

Mineral Crystal (Standard)

Hardened glass, heat-treated for scratch resistance. Used on 90% of watches in the $8-$30 wholesale range.

  • Scratch resistance: Moderate — resists daily wear but not keys or sand
  • Cost: $1-2 per piece
  • Shatter resistance: Good — won't crack easily on impact
  • Recommendation: Perfect for wholesale orders under $20/unit

Sapphire Crystal (Premium)

Synthetic sapphire — the second hardest material after diamond (9 on Mohs scale).

  • Scratch resistance: Virtually impossible to scratch in daily wear
  • Cost: $3-5 extra per piece
  • Shatter resistance: Lower than mineral — more brittle
  • Recommendation: Worth it for watches retailing above $80

Water Resistance: Understanding ATM Ratings

ATM (atmospheres) measures water pressure resistance. This is one of the most misunderstood specs in the watch industry:

Case Diameter: Sizing for Your Market

Case size dramatically affects who buys the watch. Current trends favor larger cases for men and smaller for women:

  • 38-40mm: Classic men's dress watches, vintage style. Conservative buyers.
  • 42mm: Standard men's size. Most popular across all markets. All our DS-B series uses 42mm.
  • 44-46mm: Oversized sport watches. Younger buyers, sporty demographic.
  • 32mm: Standard women's size. Elegant, understated. All our DS-W series uses 32mm.
  • 28mm: Petite women's watches. Classical jewelry-style positioning.

Strap/Bracelet Options

The strap is often the first component to wear out, so quality matters:

  • Stainless steel bracelet: Most durable, premium feel. Check for solid end links (not folded) and secure clasps with safety buttons.
  • Leather strap: Genuine leather costs $2-4 extra but lasts 1-2 years. PU leather costs $0.50 but lasts 3-6 months. Always specify "genuine leather" in your order.
  • Mesh bracelet: Milanese mesh is fashionable and adjustable. Popular in women's fashion watches. See DS-W007 Gold Mesh Bracelet.

Movement Brands to Look For

The movement is the watch's engine. Brand matters for reliability:

  • Quartz: Miyota (Japan) is the gold standard. Seiko Epson (Japan) is equally reliable. Chinese Star movements are acceptable for budget watches.
  • Automatic: Seagull (China) is the most common automatic movement in the $15-$40 wholesale range. Reliable and serviceable. Miyota 8205 (Japan) is a premium upgrade.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 316L stainless steel mean for watches?

316L is a surgical-grade stainless steel with high corrosion resistance and low nickel content, making it hypoallergenic. It is the industry standard for quality watches. Lower-grade alternatives like 304 steel or zinc alloy can cause skin irritation and corrode over time.

What is the difference between mineral crystal and sapphire glass?

Mineral crystal is hardened glass that resists scratches reasonably well and costs $1-2. Sapphire crystal is synthetic sapphire (9 on Mohs hardness scale) that is virtually scratch-proof but costs $3-5 extra per watch. For wholesale orders under $20, mineral crystal is the standard; for premium models above $30, sapphire may be worth the upgrade.

What ATM rating do I need for watches?

3 ATM (30m) is splash-resistant — fine for daily wear but not swimming. 5 ATM (50m) handles showering and brief water exposure. 10 ATM (100m) is suitable for swimming and snorkeling. For wholesale fashion watches, 3 ATM is standard and sufficient for most customers.

How do I check watch quality before bulk ordering?

Request samples, inspect case material (should be 316L steel), test water resistance at a lab, check movement branding, and examine strap/bracelet construction. All watches in our in-stock collection are pre-inspected and come with a 12-month warranty.
