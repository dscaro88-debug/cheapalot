E-Bike Market 2026: Domestic China vs Export - Where the Growth Really Is

By CheapALot Team · August 25, 2026 · 8 min read · Electric Bikes & Mobility

Here is a fact that surprises many new importers: the same Chinese factories produce two very different e-bike markets. One is a mature, price-fighting domestic market where the growth is over. The other is a fast-growing export market that set records in 2025. If you are planning to import electric bikes, choosing the right side of this split matters more than almost any other decision. This article lays out both sides with the numbers.

The Domestic Market: Mature, Regulated, Red Ocean

China's domestic e-bike market is the world's largest by volume - tens of millions of units a year - but it is a mature market fighting a price war. A new national standard (GB 17761-2024) tightened vehicle rules, added battery traceability requirements, and forced manufacturers into a compliance and consolidation cycle. Margins at the factory are thin, brands compete on price, and the days of easy domestic growth are gone.

Key takeaway: the domestic market is not where a new entrant builds a business today.

The Export Market: The Growth Engine

The export side is a different story. China exported roughly 26.7 million electric two-wheelers worth about $6.8 billion in 2025, up about 21% year on year - a new record. Growth is broad-based:

  • Europe - export value up around 23%, with the Netherlands, Poland and Germany each growing 20-35%.
  • Latin America - up around 57%, the fastest-growing continent for electric two-wheelers.
  • Africa - up around 198% on a smaller base.
  • Southeast Asia - roughly 40% of export volume, driven by cheap electrification of the region's huge motorcycle fleet.

Different regions want different products: EU buyers want 250W EPAC commuters, Southeast Asia wants commuter e-motos, Latin America and Africa want light off-road and utility models. See the categories in our Electric Bikes & Mobility range.

Same Factories, Different Bikes

The interesting part: most of this export volume comes from the same Jinhua, Tianjin and Wuxi factories that serve the domestic market. The difference is specification and certification:

CertificationCCC (GB 17761-2024)EN15194 / UL 2849 / local certs
Typical motor350-800W250W EPAC (EU) / 350-750W (US)

This is why we build every export model around destination-market compliance, not domestic specs - see E-Bike Certifications Explained.

The Honest Caveat: Not All Growth Is Profitable

Two warnings before you dive in:

  • Prices are falling - export unit prices for electric two-wheelers have been drifting down ("volume up, price down"), so margin depends on your brand, positioning and landed-cost control, not on buying cheap.
  • EU trade measures - anti-dumping duties of 9.9-70.1% and anti-subsidy duties of 3.9-17.2% apply to Chinese e-bikes until 2030. Always quote landed cost, never just FOB.

The opportunity is real, but it is an opportunity for brand-owners and distributors who control their landing cost - not for price-only players.

Where Importers Should Play

For 2026, the strongest entry points for a new importer are: EU EPAC commuter e-bikes (certification-heavy but volume-stable), US Class 1-3 e-bikes (UL 2849, higher price points), and Southeast Asian commuter e-motos (fastest electrification story). Start with OEM branding on proven models at 50-100 units, test with a mixed-container trial order, and graduate to your own brand as sales data confirms the line. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Chinese domestic e-bike market still growing?

The domestic market is mature and consolidating under the new national standard (GB 17761-2024). Volumes remain the world's largest, but growth has shifted to exports, which set records in 2025.

Which export market for e-bikes is growing fastest?

Latin America grew around 57% in 2025 and Africa around 198% on a smaller base. Europe grew about 23% in value and remains the highest-volume region for e-bikes.

Are domestic Chinese e-bikes the same as export e-bikes?

No. Domestic models are built to Chinese CCC rules; export models must meet destination requirements (EN15194 in the EU, UL 2849 in the US, local certs elsewhere). Never assume a domestic model is export-ready.

Is e-bike export still profitable in 2026?

Yes, but only with control over landed cost and positioning. Export unit prices are drifting down and EU anti-dumping duties apply until 2030, so brand-owners and distributors who control freight, duties and certification win.
