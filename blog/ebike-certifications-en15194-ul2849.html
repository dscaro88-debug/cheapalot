E-Bike Certifications Explained: EN15194 vs UL 2849 vs UL 2271

By CheapALot Team · August 25, 2026 · 8 min read · Electric Bikes & Mobility

More e-bike shipments are stopped at customs for certification failures than for any other reason. The good news: the rules are clear and knowable. The bad news: "has a certificate" and "has the right certificate for my market" are two very different things, and the gap has cost importers tens of thousands of dollars in seized or destroyed goods. Here is the certification map, market by market.

European Union: CE + EN15194 (EPAC)

To sell an electric bicycle in the EU as a bicycle (no plates, no licence), it must comply with EN15194 - the Electrically Power Assisted Cycles standard - and carry the CE mark. The hard limits: rated motor power at or below 250W, and assistance that cuts out at 25 km/h. Cross those lines and the bike is legally a motor vehicle, which changes everything downstream.

Also budget for EU trade defence measures: e-bike imports from China face anti-dumping duties of 9.9-70.1% plus anti-subsidy duties of 3.9-17.2%, in force until 2030. Always ask suppliers for a landed-cost quote that includes these.

USA & Canada: UL 2849 + UL 2271 + FCC + DOT

The US market requires battery and electrical-safety certification that is stricter than anywhere else:

  • UL 2849 - certifies the whole e-bike electrical system (drive train, battery, charger) for fire and electrical safety.
  • UL 2271 - certifies the battery pack itself for LEVs against fire and explosion hazards.
  • FCC - electromagnetic compliance for the electrical components.
  • DOT - applies where the product is classified as a vehicle.

Uncertified lithium e-bikes are seized at US borders. We never ship a US order without the UL paperwork in place.

Other Key Markets

  • Australia - RCM compliance; batteries must meet dangerous-goods transport rules.
  • Middle East - SASO / SABER certification is required before goods arrive; no valid certificate means destruction at port.
  • Southeast Asia - local certificates are mandatory: TISI (Thailand), QCVN (Vietnam), SIRIM (Malaysia). CE does not replace these.

Battery Transport: UN38.3 + MSDS + Class 9

Separate from market certification, every lithium battery must travel as dangerous goods. You need a UN38.3 test report, an MSDS, and certified Class 9 packaging. Lithium packs essentially ship by sea freight only, which is why battery paperwork gates your shipping schedule, not just your compliance. Has a Certificate vs the Right Certificate

Always ask three questions about any certificate a supplier shows you:

  1. Is it valid (not expired or revoked)?
  2. Does it cover this exact model, or a similar one?
  3. Is it recognised by your destination market?

Self-made or "decorative" lab reports are a real problem in this industry. That is why we verify certificate validity and scope with the factory before any order ships.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is EN15194 certification mandatory for e-bikes in Europe?

Yes. To legally sell an electric bicycle as a bicycle in the EU it must meet EN15194 (250W motor, 25 km/h assist cut-off) and carry the CE mark. Above those limits it is classified as a motor vehicle.

What happens if I import e-bikes to the US without UL 2849?

Uncertified lithium e-bikes are seized at customs. UL 2849 covers the whole electrical system and UL 2271 covers the battery - both are required for the US market.

Are Chinese e-bikes subject to EU import duties?

Yes. E-bikes from China face anti-dumping duties of 9.9-70.1% and anti-subsidy duties of 3.9-17.2%, currently in force until 2030. Always request landed-cost quotes.

Can one certificate cover multiple markets?

No - certifications are market-specific. CE/EN15194 does not replace UL 2849, and neither replaces RCM, SASO/SABER or TISI/QCVN/SIRIM. We map the full set for your destination.
