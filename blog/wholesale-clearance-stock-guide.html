Wholesale Clearance Stock 2026: Where to Buy Liquidation & Overstock

By CheapALot Team · August 24, 2026 · 8 min read

Wholesale clearance stock is the single fastest way to build a resale business with healthy margins — if you buy from the right channels. In 2026 the market is bigger than ever: retailers keep tighter inventories, factories still over-produce, and returns volumes keep climbing. That means more liquidation, more overstock and more clearance opportunities for buyers who know where to look.

This guide covers the main channels for buying wholesale clearance stock in the UK, EU and US, what each actually costs, and how to protect your margin. If you want the short version: buy from sources that show you real photographed stock, verify before you pay, and know your landed cost before you commit to a pallet.

What Counts as "Clearance Stock"?

Clearance stock is any inventory sold below normal wholesale price because the holder needs it gone. There are four main types:

  • Overstock — surplus production sitting in a warehouse. Factory or retailer ordered too much.
  • Customer returns — goods returned in resellable or nearly-resellable condition.
  • End-of-line / discontinued — products being replaced by new models; old stock must move fast.
  • Seasonal clearance — Christmas, summer, back-to-school stock that missed its selling window.

All four appear on wholesale markets at 30–90% below retail. The catch: quality varies wildly by source, which is why verified, photographed stock beats blind pallets almost every time.

Channel 1: UK Clearance Wholesalers

The UK has a mature clearance industry. Established wholesalers like Wholesale Clearance and similar job-lot houses sell pallets and job lots of returns, end-of-line and overstock. Prices for mixed job lots typically range from £200 to £2,000 per pallet, with per-unit costs as low as £0.10–0.50 for household goods.

What to watch: UK clearance pallets are often uninspected or "blind" — you buy a photo of a pallet, not an itemised list. Margins are still there, but your yield depends on how much is actually resellable. Ask for manifest lists, photos of individual cartons, and returns-grade vs new-grade distinction.

Channel 2: EU & US Liquidation Platforms

In the US, the major liquidation marketplaces are B-Stock, Liquidation.com and DirectLiquidation. They sell truckloads and pallets from major retailers. In the EU, similar platforms operate in Germany, France and the Netherlands, often with stricter condition grading.

The trade-off: these platforms sell large volumes (truckloads, not cartons) and buyers compete in auctions, which can push prices up. If you're buying single containers or mixed pallets in small to medium volumes, Chinese wholesale markets are often more flexible on quantity and mix.

How to Protect Your Margin

Whatever channel you choose, run this checklist before paying:

  1. Know your landed cost — unit price + freight + duties + handling. Your resale price should be 2–5x landed cost.
  2. Demand photos of actual stock, not catalogue images. If a supplier won't show current inventory, walk away.
  3. Verify the supplier — business registration, years operating, physical market address. A factory booth in Yiwu's trade city is a stronger signal than a bare Gmail address.
  4. Check the mix — for mixed cartons, ask for the category breakdown and approximate quantities per line.
  5. Budget for QC — a pre-shipment inspection (photos, count, condition check) costs a fraction of a bad container.

Container vs Pallet: Which Is Right for You?

New clearance buyers often start with pallets or job lots (£250–2,000), then move to full containers once they know what sells. A 20-foot container holds roughly 28 CBM and a 40HQ roughly 60–68 CBM — enough for 30,000–50,000 ceramic mugs, or 20,000–40,000 units of mixed small goods. Full-container pricing per unit is the lowest, and Yiwu suppliers can assemble a mixed 40HQ from multiple factories into one shipment.
