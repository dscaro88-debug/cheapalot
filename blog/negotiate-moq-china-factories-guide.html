How to Negotiate MOQ with Chinese Factories (And Still Get Low Prices)

By CheapALot Team · August 25, 2026 · 8 min read · Electric Bikes & Mobility

Minimum order quantity is the first wall every small importer hits. You have found the right product, the factory looks great - and then comes the number: "MOQ 500 units." For a first order that is too many. The good news is that MOQ is not a fixed law of physics. It is a negotiation, and it bends more than most buyers think - if you understand what it is actually paying for. Here is how to push it down without wrecking your price.

What MOQ Actually Pays For

Before negotiating, understand why the factory has an MOQ at all. MOQ covers three costs:

  • Line setup - tooling changes, jigs and calibration take time; a factory loses money if that time is spread over 20 units.
  • Materials - some components (frames, battery packs, custom moulds) have their own minimum purchase.
  • Production efficiency - small batches interrupt their line rhythm.

Once you see MOQ as "cost coverage" instead of a "rule", you know which levers work: reduce setup cost, reduce material minimums, or fill the line more efficiently.

The Five Levers That Lower MOQ

  1. Take an existing model. MOQ on a factory's standard model with simple OEM branding (logo, colour, packaging) is dramatically lower than on a custom build - typically 50-100 units instead of 500+.
  2. Mix models in one container. Many factories accept a 40HQ container split across two or three models. Your per-model MOQ drops; their line is still full. This is how smart trial orders work.
  3. Order at factory slack time. Before Chinese New Year, or in their slow season, factories are far more flexible on MOQ.
  4. Offer faster payment. A factory that gets 100% prepayment (or 30% deposit quickly) can justify a smaller first order - cash flow is a real lever, and it costs you nothing.
  5. Take standard colours and packaging. Every custom colour or box is an MOQ driver. Launch in two standard colours; add custom later.

Mistakes That Kill Deals

  • Asking for the impossible up front. "MOQ 10?" on a custom build signals a novice and raises your price. Negotiate in stages: standard model first, custom later.
  • Changing specs after the sealed sample. This voids the agreed MOQ and pricing and destroys trust. Freeze the spec before you negotiate volume.
  • Comparing apples to oranges. A $300 quote at 500 units and a $360 quote at 50 units are not comparable - compare landed cost per unit at your actual volume.
  • Burning the relationship over the last 10 units. If a factory drops from 100 to 60, take it - the difference is a rounding error on your first container.

The Trial-Order Strategy That Works

For a first e-bike order: negotiate a mixed-container trial - 50-100 units of one standard model with your logo, split into two colourways. Learn from real sales data, then scale volume to earn the better price tier.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the lowest realistic MOQ for e-bikes?

On standard models with simple OEM branding (logo, colour, packaging), 50-100 units is realistic. Custom builds with new tooling require much higher volume - typically 3,000-5,000 units.

Can I mix different models to lower per-model MOQ?

Yes. Many factories accept a container split across two or three models, which lowers your per-model minimum while keeping their line full. This is the standard way to run a trial order.

Do sample fees count toward the MOQ?

Usually the sample fee itself does not count toward MOQ, but many factories deduct the sample cost from your first bulk order. Always confirm the policy in writing before paying for samples.

When is the best time to negotiate a lower MOQ?

Factory slack seasons - typically before and after Chinese New Year and during slow demand months - are the best windows. Faster payment terms also help factories justify smaller first orders.
